## 2. Problem

Why would a final-year engineering student care?

1. **AI Anxiety & Career Uncertainty:** Engineering students across India are graduating into a radically transformed hiring market where traditional coding alone is no longer enough. Many feel unprepared, overwhelmed by theoretical AI courses, or unsure where to start.
2. **The "0-to-1" Inertia:** 90% of students stall between wanting to learn AI and actually building their first model or application. Tutorial fatigue makes the starting barrier feel insurmountable.
3. **Ad Blindness to Generic Workshops:** Engineering students see dozens of generic webinars advertised daily on social media and ignore almost all of them.
4. **Untapped Social & Campus Networks:** Engineering students operate tightly inside WhatsApp batch groups, branch forums, and college peer circles. They share opportunities when driven by **status, peer validation, and collective campus pride**.

## 3. Target Audience

* **Primary:** Final-year engineering students (2025 graduating batch and immediate 2024 graduates).
* **Secondary:** Pre-final year students (3rd year) preparing for campus placements.

* **Core Motivation:** Final-year students are actively preparing for job applications and resume building. A practical project built in 60 minutes gives them an immediate tangible resume talking point at zero financial cost.

## 5. Why I Chose This Strategy

1. **The ₹2,000 Budget Constraint:** With Meta/Google Ads CPMs targeting engineering students in India ranging between ₹150–₹350, a ₹2,000 budget yields only 6,000–12,000 impressions and at best 40–60 registrations (CAC of ₹35–₹50). Paid acquisition mathematically fails the 500-registration target.
2. **Viral K-Factor Multiplier:** In peer referral models, each registered student invites classmates. If each student brings an average of $K \ge 0.7$ friends, the organic viral loop multiplies initial seed outreach by $3\times$ to $5\times$ without marginal spend.
3. **Institutional Rivalry (Campus Pride):** Engineering colleges in India have fierce regional rivalries. Seeing a rival institution climb the leaderboard creates natural FOMO that drives organic sharing inside private WhatsApp class groups.
4. **Zero Distribution Cost:** College WhatsApp groups, Telegram study channels, and departmental student coordinators distribute links organically at ₹0 media cost.

## 9. Growth Loop Mechanics

Why is a viral growth loop superior to conventional advertising?

* **Conventional Advertising (Linear):**  
  $\text{Spend } ₹2,000 \to 50 \text{ Clicks} \to 25 \text{ Registrations} \to \text{Growth stops when budget runs out.}$
* **Campus Challenge Growth Loop (Exponential):**  
  Each cohort of students introduces the next cohort without additional ad spend:
  $$\text{Total Registrations} = N_0 \times \frac{1}{1 - K}$$
  With an initial seed of $N_0 = 150$ students and a viral coefficient $K = 0.70$:
  $$\text{Projected Registrations} = 150 \times \frac{1}{1 - 0.70} \approx 500 \text{ students}$$
  The product architecture inherently builds distribution into the user experience.

## 11. Fairness Normalization for College Size

A major challenge in campus competitions is college size asymmetry. A university with 20,000 students can easily overwhelm a specialized college with 800 students.

To maintain fairness and motivation, the leaderboard provides two toggles:
1. **Overall Registrations:** Absolute count of verified registrations.
2. **Most Engaged Campus (Participation Rate):**
   $$\text{Participation Rate} = \frac{\text{Qualified Registrations}}{\text{Eligible Final-Year Students}} \times 100$$

This allows high-density boutique engineering colleges to win the top spot through high student penetration, keeping smaller colleges engaged throughout the sprint.

4. **`quiz_results`**: Stores session tokens, individual responses, scores, and calculated tiers.
5. **`funnel_events`**: Stores touchpoint events (`visitor`, `quiz_start`, `quiz_complete`, `registration_complete`, `referral_copy`, `referral_signup`).
6. **`experiments`**: Stores A/B growth test hypotheses, variants, status, and target metrics.

## 14. Referral Logic & Integrity

1. **Code Generation:** When a student registers, the system generates a clean, readable code combining the student's name prefix and a random integer (e.g. `SUMI878`).
2. **URL Capture:** When shared (`/?ref=SUMI878`), the app automatically extracts the code from query parameters and populates the registration form.
3. **Attribution:** On submission, the backend checks that the referrer code exists in the database and is not the student's own code.
4. **Duplicate Prevention:**
   - Emails must be unique. Attempting to register an existing email returns a `400 Bad Request`.
   - Self-referrals are ignored.
   - Flagged records can be marked as suspicious in the admin CRM without deleting records.
5. **Real-time Increment:** A qualified referral immediately increments the referrer's count and the college's registration count.

## 15. Leaderboard Calculation Logic

* **College Ranking (Overall):**
  $$\text{Rank} = \text{Dense Rank of Colleges ordered by } \text{COUNT}(\text{User.id}) \text{ DESC}$$
* **College Ranking (Participation Rate):**
  $$\text{Rate} = \left(\frac{\text{COUNT}(\text{User.id})}{\text{College.eligible\_students}}\right) \times 100$$
* **Individual Champions:**
  $$\text{Rank} = \text{Users ordered by } \text{COUNT}(\text{Referral.id}) \text{ DESC}$$

## 20. The 3 Growth Tests (Copy-Paste Ready)

### Test 1: Inter-College Competition Framing vs. Generic Workshop Framing
* **Hypothesis:** Positioning the free AI workshop as an inter-college challenge (*“Which college is most ready for the AI era? Help your campus reach #1”*) will generate $>40\%$ higher visitor-to-registration conversion compared to generic workshop messaging (*“Join our free 60-minute AI workshop”*).
* **What I would test:** Split visitor traffic into Variant A (standard workshop headline) and Variant B (college competition battle headline).
* **Metric:** Visitor-to-registration conversion rate (%).

### Test 2: Leaderboard-Linked Referral Hook vs. Standard Share Link
* **Hypothesis:** Prompting registered students with explicit campus leaderboard impact (*“Invite 3 classmates to push Apex Institute into the Top 3”*) will increase the viral coefficient ($K$) from $<0.4$ to $>1.0$ compared to generic sharing copy (*“Share this workshop with your friends”*).
* **What I would test:** Test two post-registration share screens with differing copy and real-time rank impact indicators.
* **Metric:** Viral coefficient (average verified referrals per registered student).

### Test 3: Branch-Specific Domain AI Messaging vs. Generic AI Coding Copy
* **Hypothesis:** Tailoring AI value propositions to non-CSE disciplines (e.g. Mechanical: *Predictive Maintenance*, Civil: *Structural Diagnostics*, ECE: *Embedded Edge AI*) will lift non-CSE registration share from $<20\%$ to $>45\%$.
* **What I would test:** Show branch-customized application cards versus a generic Python/LLM project description.
* **Metric:** Non-CSE registration percentage and branch-wise quiz completion rate.

## 21. AI Worklog (3 Authentic Development Case Studies)

### Example 1: Defining the 0-to-1 Engineering Growth Narrative

* **Prompt:** *"Design a leaderboard algorithm that doesn't just favor massive universities with 20,000 students over smaller colleges with 1,000 students."*

* **What I Changed / Rejected:** Rejected the opaque logarithmic formula because students and recruiters cannot easily understand it. ### Example 3: Streamlining the Registration & Duplicate Prevention Flow
* **Prompt:** *"Create a referral engine with duplicate check and link generation for students."*
* **AI Output:** Proposed requiring student email OTP verification, JWT sessions, and third-party SMS verification.
* **What I Changed / Rejected:** Rejected complex auth/OTP workflows because high-friction phone verification severely depresses conversion rates for a free 60-minute workshop. Replaced it with database-level email uniqueness enforcement and clean deterministic referral codes.
